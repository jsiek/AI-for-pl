<a id="side-effect-free-instantiation"></a>
## Instantiation does not perform observable computation

### Decision

GTSFImp is designed so that instantiating a polymorphic value does not run
arbitrary computation or trigger a dynamic type error. In particular, the
body of a type abstraction `` `Λ V `` is required to be a value. Type
application may extend the type store with representation information, but
this extension is administrative: it is not an observable computational
effect and does not itself report blame.

This value restriction is reflected in `GenSafe`. A cast suspended beneath
the `gen` consistency constructor must not hide a check that should execute
before the surrounding polymorphic term becomes a value. Ground projections
and other potentially failing checks are therefore kept outside the
suspended `gen` cast.

The `inst` consistency constructor is different from such a check. It performs
polymorphic instantiation, which is side-effect free in the sense above.
Consequently, `inst` is permitted by `GenSafe` and may occur immediately
inside a suspended `gen`:

```agda
V ⟨ gen (inst c) ⟩
```

The outer `gen` remains suspended until the resulting polymorphic value is
instantiated. Delaying the enclosed `inst` by the same amount does not delay
an error, because instantiation cannot trigger one. No eager reduction rule
is needed merely to expose this `inst`.

### Operational invariant

The relevant division is therefore:

- `∀ᶜ_` is structural and may be suspended as an inert cast.
- `inst` is an instantiation step and may occur in `GenSafe` beneath `gen`.
- A ground projection or another potentially failing check is not
  `GenSafe`; it must be factored outside `gen` and performed eagerly.

This distinction preserves the intended error timing while allowing
polymorphic instantiation to remain administrative.

<a id="allocation-reveals"></a>
## Allocation reveals the instantiated result

Allocating a fresh type variable changes the representation used by the
polymorphic body. Immediately after allocation, the body still has a type
containing the fresh abstract variable, while the source redex has the
ordinary substitution instance of that body type. An upward conversion `↑`
bridges these types.

Consequently, every allocation rule that exposes a polymorphic body uses an
upward conversion generated by `〖 X ↑ A 〗`. In `β-inst`, before applying
the consistency evidence closed at `★`, the rule reveals the instantiated
body from the fresh abstract variable to `★`.

The upward conversion is administrative structure, but it is operationally
important. It recursively inserts unsealing in positive positions and
sealing in negative positions, matching the `convert↑` used at allocation
boundaries in the earlier PolyConvert development.

<a id="instantiation-closes"></a>
## Instantiation closes consistency at star

An `inst` consistency derivation relates a polymorphic body to a result type
under an environment whose fresh variable satisfies `X∼★`. At runtime,
that variable must be mediated by sealing rather than treated as a tag.
The `β-inst` rule makes this choice while the enclosing `inst` constructor is
still present.

The operation `c [ ★ /0 ]ᶜ` substitutes `★` for the bound variable in the
consistency derivation:

$$
\mathsf{inst}^{m}\ μ \;\vdash\ c : A \sim \uparrow B
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
μ \;\vdash\ c[★/0]^{c} : A[★/0] \sim B.
$$

In particular, the bound-variable consistency `X !` becomes `id ★`.
After allocating a fresh variable `α` represented by `★`, `β-inst`
first applies `〖 α ↑ A 〗` and then applies the shifted closed
consistency evidence `↑ᶜ (c [ ★ /0 ]ᶜ)`. Thus occurrences of the
inst-bound variable are implemented by the `seal α` and `unseal α`
conversions generated by `〖 α ↑ A 〗`; no variable-ground consistency
casts for `α` remain.

For example, instantiating `Λ X. λ x : X. x` through `inst` at
`★ ⇒ ★` uses the conversion `seal α ↦↑ unseal α`. A value tagged
as `ℕ` crosses the abstract boundary and returns with its original `ℕ`
tag, so the final projection succeeds.

<a id="generalization-tags"></a>
## Generalization uses fresh-name tags

A `gen` consistency derivation is not closed when its polymorphic value is
instantiated. Its fresh variable remains in consistency evidence and is an
ordinary runtime ground tag. Variable injections are therefore inert values,
and the existing tag/untag rules compare variable names just as they compare
function and base grounds.

The explicit reveal surrounding the `gen` consistency cast still transports
values through their representation types. At a negative occurrence of `X`,
the reveal and consistency structures form

$$
\mathsf{seal}\ X \; X!,
$$

while at a positive occurrence they form

$$
?X \; \mathsf{unseal}\ X.
$$

For matching names, tag/untag cancellation runs first and then seal/unseal
cancellation returns the represented value. For different fresh names `X`
and `Y`, the tag check reports blame before unsealing. Representation types
are never compared.

This distinction is binder directed: `inst` removes its fresh variable from
consistency and uses conversions, whereas `gen` retains its fresh variable as
a tag. There are consequently no general `β-tag-X` or `β-untag-X` rules.
